Dealership Logistics

How Dealerships Manage Multi-Vehicle Transport

David Chen Nov 8, 2026 6 min read

For auto dealerships, inventory logistics is a major operational expense. Moving purchased vehicles from wholesale auctions to the retail lot quickly and affordably is critical for cash flow.

The Auction Challenge Dealerships frequently buy from major auctions like Manheim or Copart. These auctions have strict rules for carrier entry and limited timeframes for vehicle removal before storage fees accrue. Dealership logistics requires specialized dispatchers who can coordinate 'gate passes' and immediate pickups.

Consolidation and Volume Loads To keep costs down, dealerships rarely ship vehicles one at a time. They utilize full truckloads (typically 9 to 10 vehicles on an open carrier). By booking a full truckload, the dealership gets a significant volume discount and a dedicated route directly to their lot, bypassing other customer drop-offs.

Inoperable Vehicles Auction vehicles are often not in perfect running condition. Carriers handling dealership logistics must be equipped with powerful winches to load 'inop' (inoperable) vehicles safely.

B2B Relationships Successful dealerships partner with reliable transport brokers or dedicated carrier networks to ensure consistent pricing and reliable transit times, keeping their lots full of fresh inventory.
